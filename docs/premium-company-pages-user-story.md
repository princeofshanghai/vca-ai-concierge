# Premium Company Pages User Story

Working user story for the Premium Company Pages full-loop prototype. Shared product context lives in [premium-company-pages.md](./premium-company-pages.md).

## Prototype Thesis

Premium Company Pages becomes more than a more credible LinkedIn Page. It becomes an intelligent growth surface that helps visitors get to the right next step and helps admins act on the demand their Page creates.

The hero story should show the full loop:

1. A visitor arrives with buying intent.
2. The visitor-facing VCA guides them toward conversion.
3. The admin-side Page Agent turns that visitor signal into insight and recommended action.

## Working Persona

**Sandy Stonehouse** is the owner of a small B2B software company.

Sandy is not a full-time marketer. He is trying to grow the business, make the company look credible, and turn LinkedIn interest into real customer conversations without building a large marketing operation.

Sandy subscribes to Premium Company Pages because he wants his Page to:

- stand out from competitors,
- earn trust quickly,
- convert visitors into inquiries,
- give him clearer signals about who is interested and why.

## Root JTBD

When I am using my LinkedIn Page to grow my business, help me turn visitor interest into qualified action so I can win customers without manually interpreting every signal myself.

## User Story

Sandy runs a small B2B software company and uses LinkedIn as one of his main credibility surfaces. His company has a Premium Company Page with a custom CTA, testimonial, credibility highlights, and AI-assisted content support.

A potential customer lands on the Page while evaluating project management tools. In the old experience, that visitor might scan the Page, click around, and leave without Sandy knowing what they needed. In the Premium Company Pages vision, the visitor-facing VCA recognizes that the visitor is evaluating the product and offers help in the moment.

The VCA answers the visitor's questions, surfaces relevant proof points, and routes the visitor to a clear next step, such as a lead form or scheduling path. The visitor does not have to hunt for the right action, and the Page does not lose the signal.

Later, Sandy opens the Premium Company Page admin dashboard. The Page Agent summarizes what happened in plain language: who visited, what they cared about, what action they took, and why the signal matters. It recommends a follow-up, suggests a content angle based on repeated visitor questions, and shows whether this pattern is emerging across similar visitors.

By the end of the story, the Page has done three things for Sandy: helped a visitor move forward, captured intent that would otherwise be invisible, and turned that intent into a concrete admin action.

## Design Insight

SMB admins do not just need more Page features. They need help turning Page activity into business outcomes.

The compelling shift is from a static Page that displays information to an agentic Page that:

- helps visitors complete their intent,
- captures demand signals,
- explains what those signals mean,
- recommends what the admin should do next.

## Experience Arc

1. Sandy has a Premium Company Page that looks more credible and conversion-ready.
2. A high-intent visitor lands on the Page while evaluating the company.
3. The VCA identifies purchase intent and starts a helpful conversation.
4. The visitor asks product-fit questions.
5. The VCA answers, shows relevant proof, and routes the visitor to a lead or scheduling path.
6. The visitor's intent, questions, and conversion action become Page intelligence.
7. Sandy opens the admin dashboard.
8. The Page Agent summarizes the visitor signal and recommends next-best actions.
9. Sandy can follow up, adjust content, improve the Page CTA, or consider promotion when the signal is strong.

## What The Story Should Prove

The prototype should help stakeholders believe that Premium Company Pages can:

- increase trust and conversion on the public Page,
- reduce friction for high-intent visitors,
- make hidden visitor intent visible to admins,
- reduce manual reporting and interpretation work,
- create a bridge from organic Page activity to paid growth motions like Boost or LinkedIn Marketing Solutions.

## Supporting Stories

The purchase-intent story is the recommended hero because it connects most directly to revenue.

Other stories can appear as secondary proof points:

- **Support intent**: VCA helps visitors resolve product or service questions.
- **Career intent**: VCA routes job seekers to relevant open roles.
- **Performance reporting**: Page Agent summarizes Page health and trends.
- **Competitor monitoring**: Page Agent identifies market shifts and content opportunities.
- **Employee amplification**: Page Agent helps coordinate employee sharing.

These should support the executive narrative without competing with the hero loop.

## UX Implications

- The visitor experience should feel helpful, not like a lead form pretending to be chat.
- The admin experience should feel strategic and concise, not like another analytics surface to decode.
- The full loop should make cause and effect obvious: visitor intent led to captured signal, which led to recommended admin action.
- Trust language needs care. Verification and credibility features should signal confidence without implying guaranteed endorsement.
- The prototype should show one strong story deeply before showing the breadth of all possible agents.

## System Implications

- Visitor-side VCA data and admin-side Page Agent data should be modeled separately, then connected through scripted prototype state.
- The first prototype can use static scripted data: visitor intent, VCA response, conversion action, captured signal, admin insight, recommended next action.
- Avoid real entitlement, verification, analytics, ads, scheduling, or lead-routing logic until the prototype scope explicitly requires it.
- Keep Premium Company Pages copy, assumptions, routes, and data separate from Hiring and member Premium.

## Open Questions

- Should Sandy's company be a fictional B2B software company, or should the prototype use a more realistic anonymized LinkedIn customer scenario?
- Should the visitor complete a lead form, schedule a meeting, or click a custom CTA as the hero conversion?
- Should the admin-side payoff be framed as a dashboard summary, proactive alert, or chat-based Page Agent response?
- How strongly should the final recommendation point toward Boost or LinkedIn Marketing Solutions?
